# Common Neurological Symptoms Guide

## Headache

### Types and Characteristics

**Tension Headache**
- Most common type (70% of all headaches)
- Bilateral, pressure-like, mild to moderate intensity
- Does not worsen with physical activity
- No nausea or photophobia typically
- Treatment: Rest, hydration, OTC analgesics (paracetamol/ibuprofen)

**Migraine**
- Unilateral, throbbing, moderate to severe
- Associated with nausea, vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia
- May have aura (visual disturbances, tingling)
- Duration: 4-72 hours
- Triggers: stress, hormones, certain foods, sleep disruption
- Treatment: Triptans, NSAIDs, antiemetics

**Cluster Headache**
- Severe, unilateral orbital pain
- Associated with ipsilateral autonomic symptoms (tearing, nasal congestion)
- Occurs in clusters, several times per day for weeks
- Rare but extremely painful

### Red Flags for Headache (Seek Immediate Care)
- Thunderclap onset ("worst headache of my life")
- Headache with fever, neck stiffness, rash (meningitis)
- Headache after head trauma
- Sudden worsening headache in patient with HIV/cancer
- Headache with neurological deficits (weakness, speech changes)
- New headache over age 50

## Neck Stiffness

Neck stiffness combined with fever and headache is a classic triad for **meningitis** — a medical emergency requiring immediate evaluation.

### Meningitis Warning Signs
- Severe headache
- High fever (>38.5°C)
- Neck stiffness (unable to touch chin to chest)
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia)
- Sensitivity to sound (phonophobia)
- Non-blanching rash (meningococcal meningitis)
- Confusion or altered consciousness

**Action: Call emergency services immediately or go to the nearest ER if these symptoms are present together.**

## Dizziness and Vertigo

**Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV)**
- Most common cause of vertigo
- Brief episodes triggered by head movement
- Caused by displaced crystals in inner ear
- Treatment: Epley manoeuvre, vestibular rehabilitation

**Vestibular Neuritis**
- Sudden onset severe vertigo lasting days
- No hearing loss, no tinnitus
- Treatment: Corticosteroids, vestibular rehabilitation

**When to seek urgent care:**
- Vertigo with sudden hearing loss
- Vertigo with headache and neurological symptoms (stroke)
- Vertigo after head trauma
